Inkling 2 Small: Thinking Machines trains a model with a native 4-bit (NVFP4) forward pass for about 2% extra compute versus MXFP8, and reports 8% faster pretraining and 20% faster RL

Lab post of Nov 13 by Jack Cook and colleagues: NVFP4 for MoE expert forward passes, MXFP8 for backward passes and the last sixth of layers, plus stochastic-rounding requantization and a Hadamard-smoothed 8-bit combine step. Pretrained in 68 hours on GB300 NVL72 racks. A secondary summary (AlphaSignal) gives 594B total / 17B active parameters on 8.4T tokens; those figures are not in the lab post.

Why it matters: The format alone is not the story: NVIDIA's earlier work found fully NVFP4 models diverge, so the per-layer, per-pass precision recipe is what makes 4-bit training usable.

Thinking Machines: End-to-End 4-Bit Training · AlphaSignal · NVIDIA: NVFP4 training

Primary (report, fetched) + TLDR AI blurb. Authors' own measurements; the report says agentic results come from training rollouts, not a held-out benchmark.

Quark: a ~165M-parameter model trained end to end for about $4,200 (about 1,300 H100-hours), with all four checkpoints released under Apache-2.0

Empero's Nov 15 report covers 500B pretraining tokens, a 16-layer hybrid with local-window and global attention that keeps the 128K KV cache near 0.5 GiB, and a GRPO-only RL variant. It scores 52.8% on a nine-task zero-shot average, best among similar-size models trained on similar budgets, though SmolLM2-135M (55.3, 2T tokens) is higher.

Why it matters: A fully documented small training stack, including failed runs in the cost, is a usable template for anyone budgeting a first pretraining run.

Embla 1: a phone-sized reasoning model averages 57.4 across five benchmarks at 4 bits and answers in 1.77 s on an iPhone 18 Pro

Sevren's Nov 2026 post reports 888 tokens per answer on average, the fewest of the reasoning models it tested. Five of twelve compared models scored higher but took 2.0x to 5.5x as long on the same phone. It trails on MATH-500 (76.1 vs about 90 for two LFM2.5 models).

Anthropic's guide to strong safety evaluations: pre-register, validate graders against human labels, and report uncertainty

An Anthropic Engineering post of Nov 13. It reports that multi-turn confidence intervals can exceed +/-10 points, that scores for one model differed by up to 34 points between the API and claude.ai, and that two models at 98% and 94% on one eval were not statistically distinguishable.

Why it matters: The same hygiene (calibrated graders, documented configs, intervals) applies to any LLM-judged eval you run on your own product.

Anthropic's 'Building always-on agents': one durable inbox, a single worker, separate timers, memory, a credential proxy and an outbox

Nov 13 article by William Hu. The agent is mostly idle and wakes per event; webhooks write to a durable inbox, one worker batches pending events, and deferred tool calls end a run and resume when the human's answer arrives as an inbox event. It also cites a 78% lower cost per solved task for Opus 5.5 versus Opus 5 on one coding benchmark.

Why it matters: The reliability work (retries, dedup, crash recovery) lives in the surrounding system, not the model loop.
