Only 45% of the AI gains Microsoft developers described were "the same work, but faster."

A new Microsoft/Oregon State study asked 331 developers what they can do now that they couldn't before. 36.9% named work that was out of reach. 12.4% named work that didn't exist before, mostly supervising agents and defining what "done" means.

Why it matters for builders: throughput charts count output. They can't tell you what AI changed, or whether your team is getting better at judging it.

The mechanism in three lines:
- Faster, Further, Afresh: three zones, and only the first shows up as a speed-up
- Developers and agents teach each other through specs, examples and tests
- The failure mode is the "hollow orchestrator": approving more work each month without being better able to judge it

Caveats: one company, self-reported, and the hollow-orchestrator risk is argued, not measured.

Question for you: what's your rule for how much agent-written change one person can approve without reading it?
