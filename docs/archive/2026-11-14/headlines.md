## Frontier Labs

1. **Gemini 4 Hydrogen ships with an Antigravity Orchestrator that Google says matches Carbon-level task success on Terminal-Bench 4.0 using 24% less quota and finishing 19% faster** (LEAD) — Google's Nov 12 post (updated Nov 13) positions Hydrogen as its fast daily-driver model at $1.50 / $7.50 per million input / output tokens with a 1M-token window and four thinking levels. Its Antigravity Orchestrator switches between Carbon and Hydrogen inside one session, with Carbon coordinating and Hydrogen running worker and verifier agents. Google does not describe the routing logic, and its own post mixes Terminal-Bench 4.0 and 4.1. Sources: [Google: Introducing Gemini 4 Hydrogen](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-4-hydrogen/) · [TLDR AI, Nov 13 (inbox)](https://tldr.tech/ai) · [TLDR Dev, Nov 13 (inbox)](https://tldr.tech/dev) · [AINews / Latent Space, Nov 14 (inbox)](https://latent.space/) *[Primary (Google blog, fetched) + TLDR AI/Dev + AINews independent spot-checks (inbox, secondhand). Benchmarks vendor-reported.]* *Why it matters: The headline gain is in the harness, not the model: the Artificial Analysis index score is 51 vs 50 for Argon. Treat the routing numbers as a hypothesis to test on your own tasks.*

2. **Anthropic previews self-hosted Claude Managed Agents: harness, sandboxes, credentials and session history inside your own environment** — Announced Nov 12 as a private beta. You operate the server, database and workers; credentials are injected by a proxy outside the sandbox, each environment can be limited to an allowlist of hosts, and every session exports a tool-call record via OpenTelemetry. Model requests can go to Anthropic, a cloud provider or your own gateway. Sources: [Anthropic: Self-hosted Claude Managed Agents](https://claude.com/resources/articles/self-hosted-claude-managed-agents) · [TLDR AI, Nov 13 (inbox)](https://tldr.tech/ai) *[Primary (Anthropic page, fetched) + TLDR AI (inbox). Private beta; no independent evaluation.]* *Why it matters: A concrete reference architecture for agent isolation: harness and secrets outside the sandbox, egress allowlists, full audit trail.*


## Research

3. **Perplexity Decider v1.3: Apache-licensed decision model answers many questions about one input in a single forward pass** — Published Nov 12. Input is a shared context plus questions with fixed answer options; output is a probability distribution per question, plus a new Locate mode that returns text spans. Perplexity reports 91.6% mean accuracy on 63 query-classification tasks (6.54 points above its production classifiers) and $26.47 per million production requests, but no matched latency comparison. Sources: [Perplexity: Decider v1.3](https://www.perplexity.ai/hub/blog/perplexity-decider) · [TLDR AI, Nov 13 (inbox)](https://tldr.tech/ai) *[Primary (Perplexity blog, fetched) + TLDR AI (inbox). Self-reported benchmarks.]* *Why it matters: Decision models are becoming a standard routing and grading primitive. Check your own labeled data; the cost figures are workload-specific.*

4. **Google's Agent Anomaly Detection flagged 87% of harmful instructions the agent actually executed, with 2%–16% false alarms and weak spots on poisoned memory** — Tested on five external benchmarks (1,774 sessions) with one ADK agent on gemini-2.5-flash. It caught 4 of 18 poisoned-memory sessions, and detection on a new agent depended heavily on how many benign sessions it had seen (33% with about 220 vs 99% with 360). It is detect-only, not blocking, and in private preview. Sources: [Google Developers Blog](https://developers.googleblog.com/how-agent-anomaly-detection-scores-on-benchmarks-we-didnt-write/) · [TLDR AI, Nov 13 (inbox)](https://tldr.tech/ai) *[Primary (Google Developers Blog, fetched) + TLDR AI (inbox). Single agent model, simulated apps.]* *Why it matters: A rare honest scorecard for agent monitoring: useful for sizing what log-based detection can and cannot catch.*

## Engineering & Tooling

7. **llama.cpp and ggml reach 1.0 with semantic versioning for the public C API** — Announced Nov 13. AINews adds that 35 deprecated functions were removed and SGLang also hit 1.0. Sources: [Hugging Face: llama.cpp v1.0](https://huggingface.co/blog/ggml-org/llama-cpp-v1) · [llama.cpp v1.0.0 release](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/releases/tag/v1.0.0) · [AINews / Latent Space, Nov 14 (inbox)](https://latent.space/) *[Primary (HF blog, fetched) + AINews (inbox). SGLang 1.0 not independently fetched.]* *Why it matters: Downstream bindings can finally pin against a stability contract instead of chasing header changes.*

8. **The No AI Slop manifesto: AIE Code speakers publish five commitments for agent-assisted work** — Modeled on the Agile Manifesto and written by AIE Code Summit speakers and attendees. It defines slop as output that costs the reader more effort than it cost the producer, and asks teams to disclose agent authorship, review what matters and encode repeated mistakes into automated checks. Sources: [slopmanifesto.com](https://www.slopmanifesto.com/) · [AINews / Latent Space, Nov 14 (inbox)](https://latent.space/) *[Primary (manifesto site, fetched) + AINews (inbox). A norms document, not a measurement.]* *Why it matters: A shareable vocabulary for code-review and agent-policy discussions at work.*
