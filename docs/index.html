Gemini 4 Hydrogen ships with an Antigravity Orchestrator that Google says matches Carbon-level task success on Terminal-Bench 4.0 using 24% less quota and finishing 19% faster

Google's Nov 12 post (updated Nov 13) positions Hydrogen as its fast daily-driver model at $1.50 / $7.50 per million input / output tokens with a 1M-token window and four thinking levels. Its Antigravity Orchestrator switches between Carbon and Hydrogen inside one session, with Carbon coordinating and Hydrogen running worker and verifier agents. Google does not describe the routing logic, and its own post mixes Terminal-Bench 4.0 and 4.1.

Why it matters: The headline gain is in the harness, not the model: the Artificial Analysis index score is 51 vs 50 for Argon. Treat the routing numbers as a hypothesis to test on your own tasks.

Primary (Anthropic page, fetched) + TLDR AI (inbox). Private beta; no independent evaluation.

Anthropic previews self-hosted Claude Managed Agents: harness, sandboxes, credentials and session history inside your own environment

Announced Nov 12 as a private beta. You operate the server, database and workers; credentials are injected by a proxy outside the sandbox, each environment can be limited to an allowlist of hosts, and every session exports a tool-call record via OpenTelemetry. Model requests can go to Anthropic, a cloud provider or your own gateway.

Why it matters: A concrete reference architecture for agent isolation: harness and secrets outside the sandbox, egress allowlists, full audit trail.

Research

Primary (Perplexity blog, fetched) + TLDR AI (inbox). Self-reported benchmarks.

Perplexity Decider v1.3: Apache-licensed decision model answers many questions about one input in a single forward pass

Published Nov 12. Input is a shared context plus questions with fixed answer options; output is a probability distribution per question, plus a new Locate mode that returns text spans. Perplexity reports 91.6% mean accuracy on 63 query-classification tasks (6.54 points above its production classifiers) and $26.47 per million production requests, but no matched latency comparison.

Why it matters: Decision models are becoming a standard routing and grading primitive. Check your own labeled data; the cost figures are workload-specific.

Primary (Google Developers Blog, fetched) + TLDR AI (inbox). Single agent model, simulated apps.

Google's Agent Anomaly Detection flagged 87% of harmful instructions the agent actually executed, with 2%–16% false alarms and weak spots on poisoned memory

Tested on five external benchmarks (1,774 sessions) with one ADK agent on gemini-2.5-flash. It caught 4 of 18 poisoned-memory sessions, and detection on a new agent depended heavily on how many benign sessions it had seen (33% with about 220 vs 99% with 360). It is detect-only, not blocking, and in private preview.

Why it matters: A rare honest scorecard for agent monitoring: useful for sizing what log-based detection can and cannot catch.

llama.cpp and ggml reach 1.0 with semantic versioning for the public C API

Announced Nov 13. Major-version bumps will now signal API/ABI breaks; the post cites 150+ architecture variants and runtime-loadable backends. AINews adds that 35 deprecated functions were removed and SGLang also hit 1.0.

Why it matters: Downstream bindings can finally pin against a stability contract instead of chasing header changes.

Primary (manifesto site, fetched) + AINews (inbox). A norms document, not a measurement.

The No AI Slop manifesto: AIE Code speakers publish five commitments for agent-assisted work

Modeled on the Agile Manifesto and written by AIE Code Summit speakers and attendees. It defines slop as output that costs the reader more effort than it cost the producer, and asks teams to disclose agent authorship, review what matters and encode repeated mistakes into automated checks.

Why it matters: A shareable vocabulary for code-review and agent-policy discussions at work.
